# Mission and authority

## Epistemic status

POLICY: the application owns permissions and the human owns approved operating goals. The offline contract is deliberately narrower than a production mission language.

A mission needs an objective, explicit component identifiers, accepted observations, permitted action kinds, approval owners, checks and evidence producers, unresolved facts, and finite run/event/worker/deadline budgets. Scope identifiers are symbolic; the simulator does not interpret them as file paths or discover a repository.

Separate the version-controlled design from approved deployment configuration. A human changing a charter is not automatically a runtime authorization update. A live adapter must authenticate the approving actor and bind approval to mission version, exact proposal, target snapshot, operation preconditions and expiry. The simulator models binding and expiry using asserted fixture identities; it cannot authenticate them.

An action allowlist is an upper bound. Approval cannot authorize an action outside it. Model text cannot expand it. Missing evidence or permission is a blocked or awaiting state, never an invitation to guess.

Keep doctrine, operational policy, model suggestions and execution history distinct. Installation may refresh hub-owned references; it must not overwrite project-approved missions, doctrine or numbered design logs. A worker must not edit its own policies or credentials.
